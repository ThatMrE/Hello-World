01 · What counts

What actually makes something
an analog astronaut mission

The phrase gets used for everything from a NASA year-long sealed habitat to a themed weekend in the desert, and that ambiguity is a real scientific problem: missions run differently cannot be pooled, so results cannot be compared and conclusions cannot be drawn. Standardisation efforts — IGSA-STD-1, released in 2023 by an international group of analog mission managers and habitat directors, and the fidelity-classification work published in the behavioural-health literature — exist precisely to fix this.

An analog is a place on Earth that resembles a target environment in some significant way. An analog mission is what happens when people go and live there as though it were that environment. The first three conditions below are necessary for anything to be called an analog astronaut mission at all; the last two are what separate a mission from a mere analog site.

The corollary. A rover trial in the Atacama, an instrument calibration on a Turkish salt flat and a suited traverse across a Canadian impact crater are all analog research, and this atlas maps them — but they are scored honestly, which means they score near zero on isolation and duration. A place can be a superb Mars analog and a poor analog mission, and the index is designed to say so out loud rather than average the distinction away.

02 · The rubric

The Analog Fidelity Index

Eight criteria, each scored 0–5 against published anchors, combined as a weighted mean and expressed 0–100. The weights below are the defaults — move the sliders and the entire atlas re-ranks in front of you, because the right weighting depends on what you are trying to study. A behavioural-health researcher should push isolation and duration; an EVA systems engineer should push suit and operations; an ISRU team probably cares about almost nothing except the ground outside.

05 · Contribute

Add a location, or correct one

This atlas is certainly incomplete. New habitats open every year, several of the 2025 World's Biggest Analog nodes are not individually documented anywhere public, and some coordinates here are the host village rather than the airlock. If you run a station, flew a mission, or simply know better — tell the atlas.

Two things happen when you submit. The record is added to your own map immediately and kept in this browser, so you can see it, filter it and export it right away. It is also sent to the site maintainers for review before it enters the public dataset — nothing you submit changes what other visitors see until a human has checked it.

Your additions

Locations you have added in this browser. They appear on the map in violet, are included in filters, ranking and every export, and never leave your device unless you submitted the form.

Known gaps

Places this atlas knows it is missing, and would like help with:

  • The French node of the 2025 World's Biggest Analog — the habitat is documented as participating, but not publicly named or located.
  • Several university and national analog habitats commissioned since 2024 that have not yet published a permanent site.
  • Exact habitat coordinates for the sites marked approx in the record panel, where the published position is a village, valley or campus.
  • Analog programmes in South and Central America, West Africa and Southeast Asia, which are almost certainly under-represented here relative to reality.

07 · Method & honesty

How this was built, and where to distrust it

What the scores are

Every score is an editorial judgement made against the published anchors in the rubric above, from public documentation of each site: operator materials, mission reports, peer-reviewed papers and press coverage. They are reproducible in the sense that the anchors are written down and the arithmetic is open — not in the sense that a second assessor would produce identical numbers. Treat them as a structured argument, not a measurement.

What the scores are not

This is not an official rating from analogstandards.space, from the IGSA-STD-1 standard, or from any habitat operator. It is an independent index built in the same spirit and informed by that work. No station has certified, reviewed or endorsed its score here. Where an operator disagrees, the operator is more likely to be right — the submission form is the place to say so.

Coordinates

Each record is marked exact or approx. Exact means a published position for the facility itself. Approx means the settlement, crater, valley or campus that hosts it, typically good to a few kilometres — deliberate in some cases, since a number of stations do not publish a precise location. Never navigate by this map.

Selection

Included: crewed analog habitats and chambers; recurring analog mission campaigns; expedition and winter-over environments used as formal spaceflight analogs; and field sites of established planetary-analog standing. Excluded: pure themed attractions with no research or training function, one-off publicity simulations, and proposed stations with no design record. Planned facilities are listed but score zero until a crew actually flies.
